The Observer Effect

Cryptography · Physics · ConsequenceNo. 04
Cover story

Everything you have ever sent is already in someone's archive.

The uncomfortable arithmetic of modern encryption is that it is not a wall but a delay. What is unreadable today becomes readable the moment the machine arrives — and the traffic has been sitting in storage the whole time, waiting patiently for it.

Which makes the interesting question not when, but how long does this need to stay secret. Answer that and the deadline computes itself.

A bigger key does not help. RSA-15360 falls to the same algorithm as RSA-2048 — it simply takes marginally longer.

The migration is not a patch. It is a replacement of the mathematics underneath every certificate, every handshake, every signed update in the estate.

Most organisations discover, somewhere in the second week of the inventory, that nobody has a list. Not a stale list — no list at all. The certificates are wherever they were put.

This is the part nobody photographs for the annual report: a spreadsheet, growing, of things that will need reissuing.

And the deadline is not the arrival of the machine. It is the arrival minus the migration, minus how long the data must survive.

Work that backwards and a comfortable 2035 becomes an uncomfortable date already behind you.

Which is, we would gently suggest, worth knowing before somebody else works it out on your behalf.
